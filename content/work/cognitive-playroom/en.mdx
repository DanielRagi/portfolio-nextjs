## Context

Cognitive Playroom grew out of research on cognitive stimulation for people with
early-stage Alzheimer's disease. The challenge was to turn clinical criteria and
research findings into a digital experience that felt clear, accessible, and
engaging.

The project began with literature review, expert interviews, and co-creation
sessions. That process identified four key areas: memory, attention, executive
function, and problem-solving.

The solution became a 3D serious game connecting multiple exercises within a
single experience. Every interaction needed to work as part of the game while
also serving a specific research objective.

## Decisions

### Design for the user

The interface could not become another barrier. Simple controls, clear
instructions, restrained environments, and direct feedback helped ensure that
the challenge came from the cognitive exercise, not from understanding the
software.

### Mechanics with purpose

Every activity started from a specific cognitive goal. The result was twelve
mini-games built around memory, attention, executive function, and
problem-solving, with variables such as time and attempts used to observe
performance.

### Lower the barrier

The game was built in Unity and delivered through WebGL to avoid installations
and reduce technical requirements. A low-poly visual direction kept the 3D
experience engaging without demanding specialized hardware.

## Outcome

The final prototype combined a 3D environment, twelve cognitive challenges,
multiple routes, variable difficulty, scoring, and reward systems. It was
then tested with people with early-stage Alzheimer's disease and their caregivers.

Participants described the experience as easy to use and entertaining, even with
little previous computer experience. The project also culminated in a scientific
publication presented at IHSI 2024.
